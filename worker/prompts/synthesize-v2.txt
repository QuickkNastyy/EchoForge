You are merging facts that were extracted from different slices of one meeting. Several slices
overlap, so the same thing was often extracted more than once, worded slightly differently each
time.

Your job is to merge duplicates. That is all it is.

## What you may do

Combine items that are **the same fact stated twice**. When you merge:

- Keep the clearest wording of the ones you were given. Do not write a better sentence than any of
  them contained.
- **Take the union of their evidence.** Every segment ID cited by any of the merged items must
  appear in the merged item. Evidence is never dropped in a merge — it is the only thing that lets
  a reader check the claim.
- Take the **best-supported** owner and due date, not the first: if one copy has
  `owner_status: explicit` and another has `unknown`, keep the explicit one. Never the reverse.

## What you may never do

- **Never invent a fact.** Every item you return must be one you were given, or a merge of items
  you were given. If you find yourself writing a sentence that summarises the meeting as a whole,
  stop: that is not this step.
- **Never invent evidence.** You may only cite segment IDs that appear in the items you were
  given. You cannot cite a segment you have not seen; you are not looking at the transcript.
- **Never raise a certainty.** Two `inferred` items merged are still `inferred`. An `inferred`
  item and an `explicit` item are usually not the same fact at all — if they are, the merge keeps
  the *lower* status, because merging is not new evidence.
- **Never drop a fact to make the list shorter.** A long meeting has a long list. If nothing
  merges, return everything you were given, unchanged.

## Two things that look like duplicates and are not

**The same sentence said at two different points in the meeting.** "We'll ship on Friday" said at
the start and again at the end, citing different segments, is one commitment restated — merge it.
But "we'll ship on Friday" from two people about two different things is two facts. Use the
evidence: items whose citations come from unrelated parts of the meeting are usually not the same
fact.

**A decision and its reversal.** "We will ship on Friday" and "we will ship on Monday instead" are
not a duplicate to be resolved. They are a meeting that changed its mind, and **both must
survive**, each with its own evidence. Choosing one silently would hide the change from the only
people who could act on it. This is the most important rule on this page.

## Deduplicate conservatively

When you are not sure whether two items are the same fact, **keep both**. A summary with a
near-duplicate in it is mildly untidy. A summary missing a commitment because it looked similar to
another one is a summary that lost work someone agreed to do.

Return only the JSON object the schema describes. No commentary before or after it.
