EchoForge · interface direction · 2026-08-05

An instrument, not a dashboard

EchoForge records two audio tracks against one monotonic clock and refuses to state anything it can't point back to. So the interface is built around time and provenance rather than around a tidy summary card.

Colour is information, never decoration. Amber is always You, teal is always Remote, red means capture and appears nowhere else. Every timecode, duration, and byte count is set in Cascadia Mono with tabular figures; prose is Segoe UI Variable, because a native Windows utility should speak the platform's voice.

02

Capturing

The signature element. Two lanes over a tick ruler, drawn from real speech density, so you can see the shape of the conversation as it happens — who is talking, who has gone quiet, where the silence is. The ruler is deliberately visible: this product is about a monotonic clock, so it shows the clock.

03

One device dropped

The plan requires that a single device failure never kills the session and is never silently patched over. The dead lane flatlines in red and stays that way in the record; the healthy lane keeps drawing. Recovery is an explicit choice, and it starts a new timeline epoch rather than pretending the gap didn't happen.

04

Out of the way

During an actual meeting the full window is noise. The compact recorder floats above the call, keeps the red indicator and the clock unmistakable, and still shows both meters so you can see at a glance that your microphone is live. Shown against dark and light grounds.

05

All recordings

Browsing is its own full-width screen, not a sidebar squeezed next to a detail pane. Each row carries what you need to decide whether to open it: the shape of the conversation, what was found in it, and whether it is finished. Rows are grouped by day, and a recording opens — it doesn't preview in place.

06

One recording, opened

The recording gets the whole window and a way back. No Actions tab: the summary is one document — overview, actions, decisions, open questions, risks — because that is exactly how the summary schema stores it, and splitting it hides half the picture. The transcript sits alongside rather than behind a tab, so a claim and its evidence are visible at the same time.

07

Following a claim to its source

The reason the two panes sit side by side. Selecting an action marks it, scrolls the transcript to the exact segment that supports it, and drops a marker on the ribbon at that moment — so checking whether EchoForge got it right takes one click and no navigation. The audio is one more click from here.

08

Processing, and falling back

Stages are named in the user's terms and carry real elapsed times. When the GPU path fails, the notice says what happened, what EchoForge switched to, and what it will cost you — the plan forbids a silent profile change, so the fallback is a statement, not a spinner.
